## Post 1 (rewrite) – Canada
**Title:** Study in Canada from Nepal in 2026: Steps, Proof of Funds and PAL
**Meta:** The 2026 steps to a Canadian study permit from Nepal: proof of funds (CAD 23,448 from 1 Sep 2026), PAL, English tests and common mistakes.
**Quick answer:** Choose a Designated Learning Institution, get a Letter of Acceptance, obtain a PAL/TAL if required, show proof of funds (CAD 23,448 for a single applicant outside Québec from 1 Sep 2026, plus tuition and travel) and apply to IRCC online.

**Sections (H2):**
1. **Step 1 – Choose a DLI and a programme.** Only DLIs can host international students. Pick programmes with a clear link to your past study and plans.
2. **Step 2 – English test.** The institution sets the score (often IELTS 6.0–6.5). Book early; results take time.
3. **Step 3 – Apply and get your Letter of Acceptance.** Keep deadlines for September, January and May.
4. **Step 4 – PAL/TAL.** Most applicants need one. Master's and PhD students at public institutions are exempt from 1 Jan 2026. Your institution requests it.
5. **Step 5 – Show your money.** CAD 23,448 + first-year tuition + travel. Use documents IRCC accepts.
6. **Step 6 – Apply, biometrics, medical.** Book biometrics at VFS Kathmandu; use an IRCC panel physician.
7. **Three mistakes that cause refusals.** Unclear funds · study plan that doesn't fit your background · inconsistent documents.

**Closing:** *Rules change often. Last checked [date]. Book a free session and we'll check your plan against today's rules.*
**Sources:** IRCC pages listed on the Canada guide.

## Post 2 (rewrite) – IELTS
**Title:** 5 IELTS Tips for a Higher Band Score (From Our Trainer)
**Meta:** Five practical IELTS tips from Reasons Education's trainer in Kathmandu: know the format, practise daily, build vocabulary, manage time and get feedback.
**Quick answer:** Learn the format, practise a little every day, use timed mock tests, build vocabulary in context and get feedback on writing and speaking.

1. **Know the format.** Four modules: Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking. Writing has Task 1 (describe data) and Task 2 (essay).
2. **Practise daily with a plan.** Work on your weakest module first. Use official practice material.
3. **Build vocabulary in context.** Read news and essays; learn how words are used, not just lists.
4. **Manage time.** Try 20 minutes for Task 1 and 40 for Task 2, with 3 minutes to check.
5. **Get feedback.** A trainer can spot patterns you can't. Our classes include written feedback and a Sunday mock test.
**Speaking tip:** Speak naturally; ask the examiner to repeat if you didn't understand.

## Post 3 (rewrite) – SOP
**Title:** How to Write a Statement of Purpose for a Student Visa (Nepal)
**Meta:** A clear structure for your SOP: your background, why this course and country, how you'll pay, and your plans in Nepal. Written in your own words.
**Quick answer:** A good SOP explains your past study, why this course and country, how you'll pay, and what you'll do next. It is written in your own voice and matches the rest of your file.

1. **Be yourself.** Don't copy templates; officers see many that look alike.
2. **Connect the dots.** How does this course follow from your past study or work?
3. **Be specific about the course and place.** Name modules or facilities you'll use and why this country.
4. **Explain gaps or weak points honestly.** Short, factual, no excuses.
5. **Show your plan, including your future in Nepal.** Name realistic roles or goals.
6. **Check consistency.** Dates, amounts and facts must match your documents.
**Our role:** *We review and coach. We don't write it for you.*
