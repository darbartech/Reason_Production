## Quick answer
You apply for a **national (Type D) student visa**, not a Schengen tourist visa. **For 2026 you must show €11,904 for 12 months (€992 per month), normally in a blocked account.**

## Snapshot
| | |
|---|---|
| Tuition | Mostly none or low at public universities, plus a semester contribution. **Some states charge non-EU students** – check the state *(DAAD)* |
| Proof of funds | **€11,904 / year** (€992/month), unchanged for 2026 *(German Federal Foreign Office)* |
| Main intakes | October (winter) · April (summer) |
| Work while studying | Limited days/hours per year for non-EU students *(confirm)* |
| After you graduate | Up to 18 months to look for work in your field |
| Language | German-taught: usually B2 or higher. English-taught: IELTS/TOEFL, score set by the university |

## Who it suits
- Students who want low-tuition public universities and can study in German or in English-taught programmes
- Students who can plan 12+ months ahead

**Think twice if:** you need to start within a few months, or you cannot meet language requirements.

## Requirements (typical)
1. Admission letter (via uni-assist or the university)
2. Proof of language (German or English)
3. Blocked account confirmation
4. Health insurance
5. Passport, certified transcripts (confirm any verification step required for Nepali documents), CV and motivation letter
6. National visa application and interview at the German embassy

## Visa steps
1. Choose a programme and check the language route
2. Prepare transcripts and certificates
3. Apply to the university
4. Receive admission
5. Open the blocked account and get health insurance
6. Apply for the national visa
7. After arrival: register address, then apply for a residence permit

## FAQ
**Do I need a Schengen visa?** No. For degree study you apply for a national (Type D) visa and then a residence permit.
**Is study free in Germany?** Many public universities charge no tuition, but you pay a semester contribution, and some states charge non-EU students. Living costs still apply.
**How much do I need in the blocked account?** €11,904 for 12 months (€992/month) in 2026.

## Requirements (typical)
1. Admission to a Japanese school or university
2. **COE** (the school applies on your behalf)
3. Passport and visa application
4. Proof of funds *(current figure from the school)*
5. Academic transcripts and certificates
6. Japanese language proof where required

## Visa steps
1. Choose a school and pathway (language school, vocational college, university)
2. Prepare Japanese language study and tests
3. Apply for admission
4. Take EJU/JLPT where the course requires it
5. School applies for the COE
6. Apply for the visa with the COE
7. After arrival: collect your Residence Card at the airport; register your address

## FAQ
**What is the COE?** The Certificate of Eligibility is issued by Japan's Immigration Services Agency, usually applied for by your school. It speeds up your visa application.
**Can I work?** Yes, with permission: up to 28 hours a week in term, more during long breaks.
**What is the EJU?** A standard test some Japanese universities use for international applicants.
**Did the rules change in 2026?** Reports mention tightened document and financial checks. We confirm the current list with your school.
